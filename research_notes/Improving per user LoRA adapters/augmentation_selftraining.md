# Data Augmentation, Self-Training and Tiny-Data RL for GUI/Web Agents with 3-5 Demos per Skill

Scope: getting more training signal out of 5 demos (about 70 step samples) for a per-user LoRA on UI-TARS-1.5-7B (Qwen2.5-VL-7B), trained with mlx-vlm on one Mac, in resettable mock mail/calendar/drive apps that have programmatic task and preference checkers and template-generated instances. Research cutoff: Nov 2026.

Verification legend: items marked **UNVERIFIED** come from prior knowledge of the paper and were not re-checked against a primary source this session. The arXiv ID is given so the writer can check it. Everything else was confirmed against the linked page during this session. Numbers from secondary summaries are flagged as such.

## Q1. Environment replay with entity substitution: is there GUI-agent precedent (OS-Genesis, AgentTrek, Explorer, Learn-by-Interact, BAGEL, NNetNav, etc.)?

### Takeaway
No GUI-agent paper I found does exactly "replay a recorded human demo on new template instances with typed values swapped." The closest published analog is robotics: MimicGen made 50K+ demos from about 200 human demos, and 10 source demos expanded to 200 nearly matched 200 human demos. The GUI synthesis line (OS-Genesis, Explorer, NNetNav, BAGEL, Learn-by-Interact, AgentTrek) shows that verified or relabeled synthetic trajectories give large gains, but those methods explore to find new tasks. They do not amplify a user's specific demos. Our environment (we own the DOM, record the element under each click, and have checkers) makes MimicGen-style replay unusually cheap and fully verifiable here.

### Cited Findings
- **MimicGen** (Mandlekar et al., CoRL 2023, arXiv 2310.17596) generated "50K+ new demonstrations for 18 tasks across 2 simulators and a physical robot arm using only ~200 source human demos." Each demo is split into object-centric subtask segments, the segments are transformed to new object poses, and only successful executions are kept. Policies trained on 200 MimicGen demos made from 10 human demos scored 79%, against 84% for 200 human demos (Square D0). — [MimicGen arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.17596); [project site](https://mimicgen.github.io)
- **OS-Genesis** (arXiv 2412.19723, ACL 2025) does "reverse task synthesis": the agent first explores and acts step by step, then derives tasks retrospectively, and a trajectory reward model filters for quality. On AndroidWorld it nearly doubles task-driven synthesis, from 9.82% to 17.41%. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.19723); [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.277)
- **Explorer** (Pahuja et al., arXiv 2502.11357, Findings of ACL 2025) built 94K+ successful multimodal web trajectories (49K URLs, 720K screenshots, 33M elements) at an average cost of $0.28 per successful trajectory, using exploration-driven refinement of abstract task proposals. It reports strong results on Mind2Web-Live, Multimodal-Mind2Web and MiniWob++, and argues data scaling is a key driver. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.11357); [ACL Findings](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-acl.326)
- **NNetNav** (Murty et al., arXiv 2410.02907, ICLR 2025) uses an exploration policy plus a relabeler, built from the same zero-shot LLM, that periodically checks whether the trajectory so far can be given a meaningful instruction and prunes exploration when it cannot. Llama-3.1-8B fine-tuned on 10k self-generated demos gets over 16% on WebArena and 35% on WebVoyager. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.02907v2)
- **BAGEL** (arXiv 2403.08140) turns random exploration into executable, meaningful trajectories through an iterative round trip between two LM components. The authors compare it to Hindsight Experience Replay, but the relabeling works on the whole transition history rather than only the final observation. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.08140v2). BAGEL's reported ICL gains (about 2-13% absolute on ToolQA/MiniWoB++) are **UNVERIFIED**.
- **Learn-by-Interact** (arXiv 2501.10893, ICLR 2025) synthesizes trajectories from interaction and documentation. Its "backward construction" uses LLMs to write an instruction for each sub-trajectory, which gives "up to 10.6% improvement for training". Evaluated on SWE-bench, WebArena, OSWorld and Spider2-V. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.10893); [ICLR 2025](https://www.iclr.cc/virtual/2025/poster/31073)
- **AgentTrek** (arXiv 2412.09605) harvests web tutorials and runs a VLM agent through them in a "guided replay" to produce trajectories. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.09605v2). The cost figure of about $0.55 per trajectory is **UNVERIFIED**; the search snippet was cut off before the cost section.
- **Agent Workflow Memory** (Wang, Mao, Fried, Neubig, arXiv 2409.07429) induces reusable workflows from a few examples by abstracting example-specific values into variables, which is conceptually the same as entity slotting. The reported relative gains of about 24.6% on Mind2Web and 51.1% on WebArena are **UNVERIFIED**.
- **Synatra** (arXiv 2409.15637) turns indirect knowledge such as tutorials into synthetic demonstrations at scale. **UNVERIFIED** (not re-checked).

### Inferences
- Treat each demo as a MimicGen-style program. Segment it at the recorded element interactions. Store each click as (role/selector/text of element, relative offset), not as raw pixels. Store each typed string as a slot such as `{recipient}`, `{date}` or `{filename}`, found by matching the typed text against the template instance's entities. On a new instance, re-resolve every element by selector in the live DOM, compute the new bbox centre in UI-TARS coordinate space, type the substituted values, and capture fresh screenshots. Keep a replay only if both the task checker and the preference checker pass. This is MimicGen's "transform, execute, keep successes" loop. In our setting it needs no learned model and produces verified labels.
- This targets the "dates wrong" failure directly: 70 samples contain only about 5 date values, and replays over 50+ instances show the model dozens of (instruction date, typed or picked date) pairs, including date-picker navigation across months.
- Keep demo-specific preference steps (the "skipped user steps") in every replay. Because every replay passes the preference checker, the preference steps go from 5 occurrences to hundreds.
- Exploration-and-relabel methods (OS-Genesis, NNetNav, BAGEL) are a weaker fit for per-user adapters. They create new tasks the user never demonstrated, so the resulting trajectories follow the base model's habits rather than the user's preferences. One part is still useful: **hindsight relabeling of failed learner rollouts**. Run every template checker on the final state of a failed episode. If the episode accidentally completed another valid instance (for example, it archived the wrong email) and also passed the preference checker, keep it as a positive example for that instance.
- Expected limitation: replays have the same action sequence as the source demo. They add visual and entity diversity, not diversity of behaviour. They will not teach recovery from off-demo states (see Q5).

### Gaps
- No GUI/web paper was found that explicitly reports "demo replay with entity substitution" or "trajectory editing" as an augmentation with an ablation. The term "trajectory editing" turned up nothing specific in GUI agents. That evidence for our approach comes by analogy (MimicGen, AWM).
- No paper found reports how many augmented replays per human demo saturate gains for a VLM GUI policy. MimicGen's 10-to-200 (20x) ratio is the only quantitative anchor, and it comes from robotics.
- AgentTrek's per-trajectory cost and BAGEL's numbers were not verified.

## Q2. Instruction paraphrase augmentation and synthetic state perturbation (layout jitter, scrolled states)

### Takeaway
There is little direct GUI-agent evidence that isolates paraphrase or layout jitter. The supporting evidence is indirect: backward construction and relabeling (Learn-by-Interact, OS-Genesis, NNetNav), and classic VLN augmentation (Speaker-Follower back-translation, EnvDrop). For us both are nearly free, because we control the templates and the CSS. They should be applied on top of the replays, not to the raw 70 samples alone.

### Cited Findings
- Writing (or rewriting) instructions for sub-trajectories after the fact is the central lever in Learn-by-Interact (up to 10.6% training gain from backward construction) and in OS-Genesis (reverse task synthesis, 9.82% to 17.41% on AndroidWorld). — [Learn-by-Interact](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.10893); [OS-Genesis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.19723)
- NNetNav's relabeler writes instructions for exploration prefixes and prunes the ones it cannot label, so instruction diversity comes from relabeling rather than human writing. — [NNetNav](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.02907v2)
- VLN precedents: Speaker-Follower (Fried et al., arXiv 1806.02724) uses a speaker model to back-translate synthetic instructions for augmentation. EnvDrop (Tan, Yu, Bansal, arXiv 1904.04195) uses environmental dropout to synthesize "new environments" for robustness. Both are **UNVERIFIED** (not re-checked this session).
- UIShift/GUI-Shift (arXiv 2505.12493, ICLR 2026) shows that transition pairs (screenshot_t, screenshot_{t+k}) can supervise the model by asking for the action that caused the change. This turns existing screenshots into extra self-supervised samples (about 2K samples, Qwen2.5-VL-3B/7B, up to 11.2% gain in GUI automation accuracy). — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.12493v3); [ICLR 2026 poster](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2026/poster/10009851)
- "The Next Screenshot Knows: Gated Hindsight Distillation" (Li et al., arXiv 2608.06065, Aug 2026) argues that the evidence for an action "often appears only on the subsequent screen". It uses a parameter-sharing teacher that also sees the next screenshot to re-score the student's on-policy responses, and distills only when the student fails and the hindsight teacher recovers the demonstrated action. It reports gains over GRPO on AndroidWorld and AndroidLab; the abstract gives no numbers. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06065)

### Inferences
- **Paraphrase:** for each template, generate about 5-10 instruction surface forms, using an LLM or hand-written variants: synonyms, reordered clauses, relative vs absolute dates ("next Tuesday", "on the 14th", "Dec 14"), and nicknames vs full names. Store the resolved canonical values so the replay types the correct value. Make sure paraphrases never state the user's preference: the adapter has to learn to apply it without being told. Relative-date paraphrases with the "today" date in the prompt directly train the date-resolution failure.
- **State perturbation in our own apps:** render the same instance under (a) 2-3 viewport sizes or zoom levels, (b) shuffled list order and extra distractor items (more emails, events, files), (c) pre-scrolled states where the target is below the fold, so the correct first action is `scroll`, and (d) theme or font variants. Because clicks are replayed by selector, labels stay correct automatically. Re-run the checker on each variant.
- **Auxiliary inverse-dynamics samples** (UIShift-style) from the same recordings: (s_t, s_{t+1}) to a_t. These cost nothing extra and give state-change awareness, which may help with the "repeat previous action / where am I" loop. Our instance is the mock app, so we can also compute exact transition labels.
- Watch the token budget: a 1280x800 screenshot is about 1.3K visual tokens in Qwen2.5-VL (28x28 px per merged token, by my calculation). Viewport jitter changes the token count, so adding history screenshots can push past `max_seq_length` (see Q4).

### Gaps
- No GUI-agent study was found that isolates the effect of instruction paraphrase alone, or of layout/CSS jitter alone, on success rate. Expected effect sizes are unknown.
- Speaker-Follower and EnvDrop numbers were not re-verified.

## Q3. Self-generated trajectories filtered by a checker: RFT, STaR, ReST/ReST-EM, filtered BC, Patel et al., WebRL, PAE, Agent Q. How many rollouts, and what gains from tiny seeds?

### Takeaway
Filtering self-generated trajectories with a success signal and fine-tuning on the survivors reliably improves web agents. Examples: about 31% relative improvement on WebArena with no new human data (Patel et al.); 4.8% to 42.4% with online curriculum RL (WebRL); 18.6% to 81.7% on real booking after one day of self-collected data (Agent Q). Our checkers are exact rather than LLM judges, which removes the main noise source these papers fight. Each round is limited by the adapter's starting success rate: with 5 demos, start this only after the replay-SFT stage has lifted the base pass rate.

### Cited Findings
- **Patel et al., "Large Language Models Can Self-Improve At Web Agent Tasks"** (Patel, Hofmarcher, Leoveanu-Condrei, Dinu, Callison-Burch, Hochreiter, arXiv 2405.20309). The base model generates trajectories, and any trajectory where the model self-detected failure or where failure was visible in the environment is filtered out. Fine-tuning on three synthetic mixtures gives a 31% improvement in task completion over the base model on WebArena. The paper also tests whether the model can invent new tasks, pages and solution trajectories. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.20309v2); [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2405.20309). The base model (Qwen-1.5-72B-Chat) is **UNVERIFIED**.
- **WebRL** (Qi, Liu et al., arXiv 2411.02337, ICLR 2025) uses a self-evolving curriculum that generates new tasks from unsuccessful attempts, plus an outcome-supervised reward model and adaptive RL. Llama-3.1-8B goes from 4.8% to 42.4% on WebArena-Lite. The 70B figure appears as 47.3% in one version and 49.1% in v3 (inconsistent across versions); GLM-4-9B goes from 6.1% to 43%. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.02337); [ICLR](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/c66e1fcc9691aae706250638f36f681b-Abstract-Conference.html)
- **PAE** (Zhou, Yang, ... Levine, Li, arXiv 2412.13194, ICML 2025): a context-aware task proposer, an agent, and a VLM success evaluator that serves as the RL reward. It reports about 30% relative improvement in average success (arXiv) and about 50% relative improvement in zero-shot generalization (ICML abstract). LLaVA-1.6-7B with PAE matches LLaVA-1.6-34B fine-tuned on demonstrations. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.13194); [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/zhou25ah.html)
- **Agent Q** (Putta et al., arXiv 2408.07199) combines guided MCTS with self-critique and iterative fine-tuning using an off-policy DPO variant. In real-world booking it takes Llama-3-70B from 18.6% zero-shot to 81.7% after "one day of data collection", and to 95.4% with online search. On WebShop it beats BC and RFT baselines. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.07199). Secondary sources say the booking site is OpenTable, which the abstract does not state.
- **Classic self-training references**, all **UNVERIFIED** (well known, not re-checked): STaR (Zelikman et al. 2022, arXiv 2203.14465), which keeps self-generated rationales that reach the correct answer, iterates, and "rationalizes" failures given the answer as a hint. ReST (Gulcehre et al. 2023, arXiv 2308.08998), with Grow (sample) and Improve (filter by reward threshold, then fine-tune) loops. ReST-EM (Singh et al. 2023, "Beyond Human Data", arXiv 2312.06585), which is EM-style binary-reward-filtered self-training; it beat human-data fine-tuning on MATH/APPS with PaLM-2, and gains taper after a few iterations with some overfitting. RFT (Yuan et al. 2023, arXiv 2308.01825), which is rejection sampling fine-tuning where more distinct correct reasoning paths help.
- **ARPO** (arXiv 2505.16282) adds a replay buffer to GRPO that keeps successful trajectories per task and injects one when a GRPO group has only failures. It reports 29.9% on OSWorld. A secondary source says the buffer raised in-domain success from 68.8% (plain GRPO) to 81.25%. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.16282); [GitHub](https://github.com/simular-ai/arpo)
- **ZeroGUI** (arXiv 2505.23762) has a VLM generate tasks and estimate rewards, then runs two-stage online RL on UI-TARS and Aguvis on OSWorld and AndroidLab with no human labels. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.23762); [GitHub](https://github.com/OpenGVLab/ZeroGUI)
- **SEAgent** (arXiv 2508.04700), a self-evolving computer-use agent that learns novel software from its own experience. **UNVERIFIED** (not re-checked).

### Inferences
- In our setting, RFT/filtered BC means: sample k rollouts per new template instance from the current adapter at temperature 0.6-1.0, and keep an episode only if the task checker and the preference checker both pass. Optionally keep only the shortest successful episode per instance to suppress loops and redundant actions. Mix the kept episodes with demos and replays, re-SFT, and repeat for 2-3 rounds; ReST-EM-style tapering is expected after that (UNVERIFIED).
- Our exact checkers remove the evaluator noise that Patel (self-critique) and PAE/ZeroGUI (VLM judge) have to cope with, so filtered BC should be cleaner here than in those papers.
- Cheap template instances make the WebRL curriculum trivial: oversample templates and entity types the current adapter fails (for example, dates in another month, or long recipient lists).
- For instances where all k rollouts fail, inject the scripted or replayed demo for that instance, following the ARPO/BEPA idea, so that hard instances still contribute signal.
- Rollout count: there is no published "minimum rollouts from a 5-demo seed" number. A practical budget is 50-100 instances x k=4 = 200-400 episodes per round. At about 14 steps x 2.5 s, that is about 2-4 h of Mac time per round (see Q6).

### Gaps
- None of the confirmed sources give rollouts-per-task or total rollout counts in the snippets I checked (Patel, WebRL, PAE, Agent Q). The full papers would need to be read.
- No source starts self-training from as few as 5 demos for a VLM GUI agent. All web self-improvement papers start from a strong zero-shot base or a large LLM.

## Q4. RL with verifiable rewards on tiny data for GUI agents (UI-R1, GUI-R1, InfiGUI-R1, GUI-G1, UIShift); step-level rewards; GRPO + LoRA; feasibility with mlx-vlm on one Mac

### Takeaway
Single-step, rule-rewarded GRPO works with very small GUI datasets. UI-R1 used 136 tasks with Qwen2.5-VL-3B (+15% action-type and +10.3% grounding accuracy on AndroidControl), and GUI-R1 used 3K samples. LoRA appears to be sufficient for RL: Thinking Machines report LoRA matches full fine-tuning for RL even at very low rank. mlx-vlm now ships a GRPO trainer (`GRPOTrainingArgs`, custom reward functions, LoRA/DoRA, `beta=0` without a reference model), but its docs do not mention multi-turn or agentic rollouts. The feasible path on one Mac is therefore **step-level GRPO** on screenshots from demos, replays and on-policy states, rewarded by matching against the recorded element. Full episode-level online RL would require writing our own loop.

### Cited Findings
- **UI-R1** (Lu et al., arXiv 2503.21620, AAAI). v1 abstract: "a small yet high-quality dataset of 136 challenging tasks, encompassing five common action types on mobile devices." UI-R1-3B (base Qwen2.5-VL-3B) improves AndroidControl action-type accuracy by 15% and grounding by 10.3%, beats the base by 6.0% on ScreenSpot-Pro, and is competitive with OS-Atlas-7B, which was SFT-trained on 76K samples. The reward is rule-based: action type + action argument + format, optimized with GRPO. Later versions (AAAI) use a 2K-task set and report average gains of 18.3% on ScreenSpot, 6.0% on ScreenSpot-Pro and 10.9% on AndroidControl. — [arXiv v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.21620v1); [later version](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.21620v5); [AAAI](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/38816)
- **GUI-R1** (arXiv 2504.10458): an R1-style generalist GUI action model with a rule-based unified action-space reward, trained on GUI-R1-3K. It claims to beat OS-Atlas using about 0.02% of the data (3K vs 13M, cited as 14M in another version). — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.10458v4)
- **GUI-G1** (arXiv 2505.15810): R1-Zero-style grounding with 17K public samples on Qwen2.5-VL-3B, reaching 90.3% on ScreenSpot and 37.1% on ScreenSpot-Pro. It notes that InfiGUI-R1's "slow thinking" template hurts grounding as reasoning gets longer. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.15810v2). Two further GUI-G1 findings are **UNVERIFIED**: box-size reward hacking and GRPO length bias.
- **InfiGUI-R1** (arXiv 2504.14239), an Actor2Reasoner framework built on Qwen2.5-VL-3B. Its data size and numbers were not found this session (**UNVERIFIED**).
- **UIShift/GUI-Shift** (arXiv 2505.12493, ICLR 2026): one-stage GRPO on about 2K self-supervised transition samples (inverse dynamics), Qwen2.5-VL-3B/7B. It is competitive with or better than SFT baselines on ScreenSpot and AndroidControl, with up to 11.2% gain in automation accuracy. The authors' rationale is that several action parameters can be functionally equivalent and produce the same next state, which SFT handles poorly. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.12493v3)
- **One-shot RLVR** (Wang et al., arXiv 2504.20571, NeurIPS 2025): a single training example lifts Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B on MATH500 from 36.0% to 73.6% (six-benchmark average from 17.6% to 35.7%). Test accuracy keeps improving after training accuracy saturates ("post-saturation generalization"), and the effect holds for GRPO and PPO on several models. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.20571v3); [MSR page](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/reinforcement-learning-for-reasoning-in-large-language-models-with-one-training-example/). Caveat, **UNVERIFIED**: later "spurious rewards" work (arXiv 2506.10947) suggests Qwen models gain from RL even with weak rewards, so some tiny-data RL gains may be model-prior effects.
- **BEPA** (Wang et al., arXiv 2601.05787, ACL 2026) uses **UI-TARS-1.5-7B, our exact base**. Level 1 re-rolls expert plans under the base policy to get policy-compatible trajectories. Level 2 keeps a per-task cache of the policy's own successes, injected into GRPO only when on-policy exploration fails. OSWorld-Verified goes from 22.87% to 32.13%, and a held-out split from 5.74% to 10.30%. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.05787v1); [ACL](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.1082/)
- **LoRA + RL:** "LoRA Without Regret" (Schulman et al., Thinking Machines, Sep 2025) finds LoRA performs equivalently to full fine-tuning for RL even at small ranks, and recommends a higher LR than full fine-tuning and an effective batch size under 32. For SFT, LoRA underperforms once the dataset exceeds LoRA's capacity. The TRL guide recommends RL LoRA ranks of 1-32. — [Thinking Machines blog](https://thinkingmachines.ai/blog/lora/); [TRL docs](https://huggingface.co/docs/trl/main/en/lora_without_regret). The rank-1 GRPO match on MATH/GSM comes from secondary write-ups.
- **mlx-vlm GRPO (official docs):** the README says "MLX-VLM supports VLM SFT, ORPO, DPO, and GRPO with LoRA, QLoRA, DoRA, or full fine-tuning." Training runs through `python -m mlx_vlm.train` or the Python API (`Trainer`, `VLMTrainingArgs`, `prepare_model_for_training`). — [mlx-vlm README](https://github.com/Blaizzy/mlx-vlm)
- From mlx-vlm `docs/training.md` — [training guide](https://github.com/Blaizzy/mlx-vlm/blob/main/docs/training.md):
  - `GRPOTrainingArgs` exposes `group_size` (4 in the example), `max_completion_length` (128), `max_seq_length` (4096), `beta` (0.0), `grad_checkpoint`, `logit_chunk_size`, `importance_sampling_level` (`token`/`sequence`), `epsilon_high`, `advantage_epsilon`, and loss types `grpo` / `bnpo` / `dr_grpo`.
  - `beta=0` skips the reference model. `beta>0` needs a separate frozen `reference_model`.
  - Reward callbacks receive `prompts`, `completions`, `answer`, `types`, `metadata` and return one float per completion (`None`/NaN means not applicable). They combine through `reward_weights`; the CLI flags are `--reward-functions`, `--reward-functions-file`, `--reward-weights`.
  - Advantages use per-prompt group standard deviation, so equal rewards give zero advantage; the docs say to monitor `grouped_reward_std`.
  - `train_type` is `lora`/`dora`/`full`. `target_modules` can include vision targets. 4-bit or 8-bit quantization is optional.
  - The docs say to keep adapter dropout at zero for consistent rollout and scoring probabilities.
  - Stated limitations: no audio or video GRPO; each rollout is used once ("not multi-epoch PPO or DAPO dynamic resampling"); prompt, image and generated tokens all count toward the sequence limit, and oversized prompts raise an error instead of being truncated.
  - Multi-turn or agentic rollouts are not mentioned.
- The mlx-vlm vision GRPO example is a notebook (`grpo_vision.ipynb`) running Qwen3.5-2B-4bit on LaTeX OCR with dense similarity and exact-match rewards. The README says the tested code paths use tiny models and fixtures, not default Hub-model runs. DPO loads a policy and a frozen reference; ORPO is reference-free. — [mlx-vlm examples/training README](https://github.com/Blaizzy/mlx-vlm/blob/main/examples/training/README.md)
- A third-party Apple-Silicon option, mlx-tune, offers a `VLMGRPOConfig` with LoRA on vision and language layers. mlx-lm-lora has `--train-mode grpo`, but it is text-only. — [mlx-tune VLM docs](https://arahim3.github.io/mlx-tune/vlm.html); [mlx-lm-lora](https://github.com/Goekdeniz-Guelmez/mlx-lm-lora)

### Inferences
- **Step-level GRPO design for us (UI-R1-style):** each prompt is one state (instruction + screenshot + action history in the exact inference format), taken from demo steps, replay steps and learner-visited states (Q5). Reward functions, written in `rewards.py`:
  - action-type match (1/0);
  - for clicks, whether the predicted point falls inside the recorded element's bbox (we record the element under each click, so the bbox is exact), with an optional dense term that decays with distance to the centre;
  - for `type`, exact or normalized match against the slot value (directly targets date errors);
  - for `scroll` and `hotkey`, direction or key match;
  - a parse/format reward;
  - optionally, a bonus weight on "preference steps" so skipped user steps get extra gradient.
  - Set `beta=0` (no reference model, halving memory), `group_size` 4-8, `max_completion_length` about 64-128 (keep UI-TARS "Thought" short, per GUI-G1's finding that long reasoning hurts grounding), and LoRA rank 8-32 with dropout 0.
- **Prompt filtering:** GRPO advantages are zero when all G samples get the same reward, so pre-screen prompts. Sample G actions from the current adapter and keep only states with mixed outcomes. Following ARPO/BEPA, for all-fail states either drop them (pure GRPO) or route them to SFT with the oracle label.
- **Equivalent-action tolerance:** UIShift's point that several parameters can produce the same next state supports bbox-hit rewards over exact-coordinate SFT targets. This is one concrete advantage of GRPO over SFT for our grounding errors.
- **Episode-level RL** (WebRL/ARPO/ZeroGUI-style, reward from the end-of-episode checker) is not covered by mlx-vlm's trainer. It would need a custom loop: roll out episodes in the env, then for each step build a (prompt, completion) pair with the episode advantage and call the GRPO loss manually. Given 2-3 s per step and sequential generation, episode-level groups (G=4 episodes x 14 steps, about 2.5 min per group) are slow but feasible overnight for about 50-100 groups. Treat this as an optional stretch.
- **Memory and sequence:** UI-TARS-7B in bf16 is about 15 GB of weights, or about 4-5 GB at 4-bit (my estimate). With `beta=0` there is no reference copy. One screenshot is about 1.3K tokens at 1280x800, so with 1-2 history screenshots, raise `max_seq_length` above 4096 or drop history images. Otherwise mlx-vlm errors rather than truncating.

### Gaps
- mlx-vlm has no published GRPO timing or memory figures for 7B VLMs, and no confirmation that its GRPO path has been tested on Qwen2.5-VL/UI-TARS checkpoints (the README notes tests use tiny models). The trainer's `processing_class` also needs to handle UI-TARS's prompt template; this needs a smoke test.
- It is unconfirmed whether mlx-vlm batches the G completions in one generate call or runs them sequentially. That determines whether step cost is about G x 2.5 s or less.
- InfiGUI-R1's data size and results, and GUI-G1's reward-hacking and length-bias findings, were not verified.
- No GUI paper was found that runs GRPO on as few as about 70 step samples from one user. UI-R1's 136 tasks is the closest, and it is a mobile benchmark, not personalized.

## Q5. Recovery data: DAgger-style or on-policy correction to fix error compounding that teacher-forced SFT ignores

### Takeaway
Through 2026 the field converged on training on learner-visited states with expert or verifier corrections: DAgger revivals, rollback correction, reflection tuning and recovery-trajectory synthesis. Our environment is resettable, template-driven and programmatically checkable, so we can build a **state-conditional scripted oracle** per skill and run DAgger with nearly free labels. This is the most direct fix for the "repeat previous action / where am I" and "skips the user's steps" failures, which are classic covariate-shift symptoms.

### Cited Findings
- **"Revisiting DAgger in the Era of LLM-Agents"** (arXiv 2605.12913, May 2026) describes DAgger as training on states the learner is likely to visit, with no-regret guarantees that improve imitation error from quadratic to linear in the horizon. It reports gains on SWE-bench Verified with 4B and 8B models. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.12913v1) (from a search summary; full paper not read). Original DAgger: Ross, Gordon, Bagnell, AISTATS 2011, arXiv 1011.0686 (**UNVERIFIED**, not re-checked).
- **Speculative Rollback Correction (SRC)** (Hao et al., arXiv 2606.12485, Jun 2026) is described as DAgger-style correction for web agents in resettable environments. The student runs a fixed-horizon speculative segment; the teacher marks the first harmful deviation only when local progress breaks down; rollback keeps the useful prefix. Verifier-passing rollouts are archived for quality and diversity. On WebArena-Infinity it collected 977 verifier-passing trajectories and 9,183 next-action examples, and claims fixed-horizon review beats step-level review on the recovery-versus-query tradeoff. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.12485)
- **"A Few Teacher Steps Go a Long Way"** (Ye et al., arXiv 2607.04574, Jul 2026): at matched supervision budgets, bounded, unfiltered teacher continuations from learner-induced contexts beat pure behaviour cloning, and match or beat success-filtered alternatives on HotpotQA and ALFWorld (also Terminal-Bench). It is not GUI, and the abstract gives no numbers. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.04574)
- **Scale AI, "on-policy expert corrections"** (SWE agents): purely on-policy training data can be unstable on long-horizon tasks, and classic DAgger queries only a single corrective action, which makes trajectory-level rewards hard to use. — [Scale Labs blog](https://labs.scale.com/blog/training-robust-multi-turn-lm-agents-with-on-policy-expert-corrections)
- **RoTS / GUI-RobustEval** (Bu et al., arXiv 2605.29447, ICML 2026 Spotlight): a benchmark of 1,216 executable error-recovery cases, plus tree-based "Robustness-driven Trajectory Synthesis" that discovers error modes and synthesizes about 800K recovery examples. RoTS-32B reaches 47.4% on OSWorld (33.8% All-Pass@4). — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.29447)
- **GUI-Reflection** (arXiv 2506.08012, NeurIPS 2025): its offline stage builds reflection and error-correction data from existing successful trajectories with no human annotation, and its online reflection tuning generates "pre-error correction" and "post-error reflection" supervision from the model's own interactions. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.08012v1); [NeurIPS](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/937ae0e83eb08d2cb8627fe1def8c751-Abstract-Conference.html)
- **UI-TARS** (arXiv 2501.12326) collects "reflective online traces" on hundreds of VMs, filters and refines them, and iterates with reflection tuning so the model learns from its mistakes. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.12326v1). The claim that UI-TARS used DPO on (error, corrected) pairs is **UNVERIFIED**; the search did not confirm it.
- **BEPA's** Level-1 step (re-rolling expert plans under the current policy) is another way to convert off-policy demos into on-policy-compatible data, demonstrated on UI-TARS-1.5-7B. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.05787v1)
- **Gated Hindsight Distillation** distills only on states where the on-policy student fails but a hindsight teacher (with the next screenshot) recovers the demo action, which is a form of targeted on-policy correction. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06065)

### Inferences
- **Scripted oracle = free DAgger expert.** For each skill, turn the aligned demos into a state-conditional program. It reads mock-app state (DOM or backend: is compose open? is the recipient filled? is the date set? has the user's preference step been done, such as label applied, CC added, or folder chosen?) and returns the next correct action, including the user's preference steps, with bbox from the live DOM. This is the same engine as Q1's replay, with progress detection added. Run the learner on new instances (temperature 0, cap about 25 steps), label every visited state with the oracle action, aggregate (DAgger), and re-SFT. Weight states where learner and oracle disagree above agreeing states.
- **Repeat-action loops:** visited states after a repeated action produce training examples whose correct label differs from the previous action. Also add ORPO/DPO pairs (rejected = "repeat previous action", chosen = oracle action) at those states. ORPO is reference-free in mlx-vlm, which saves memory.
- **SRC-style budget saver:** if the oracle is expensive or partial, review fixed-horizon segments (for example 3 steps), roll back to the first harmful deviation, and keep the prefix. Our resettable apps support this directly.
- **Synthetic recovery without rollouts (GUI-Reflection/RoTS offline idea):** from demo or replay states, inject a perturbation (click a wrong but plausible element, open the wrong email, pick the wrong date, or repeat the last action), capture the resulting screenshot, and label the recovery (back, close, undo, re-select) followed by the continuation. This is cheap and targeted at our three failure modes.
- **Prompt and format check (my inference, not from a paper):** "where am I" repeats often indicate a train/inference mismatch in how action history is presented. Make sure the SFT prompt includes the same history (previous actions and/or screenshots) used at inference, and that history is rebuilt from the agent's own actions during DAgger rollouts.

### Gaps
- No GUI paper found reports DAgger with a programmatic oracle in synthetic web apps, though the approach follows directly from the theory. No published effect size exists for our scale (about 70 seed steps).
- SRC's and "Revisiting DAgger"'s exact gains over SFT were not in the abstracts read.
- UI-TARS's exact reflection-tuning objective (DPO or SFT) was not confirmed.

## Q6. Ranked recipe for our setup (UI-TARS-1.5-7B LoRA, mlx-vlm, one Mac, 5 s per train step, 2-3 s per agent step) with hours and compute

### Takeaway
Rank by signal per Mac-hour. (1) Checker-verified, entity-substituted replays plus layout and paraphrase variants, then SFT. (2) DAgger with a scripted state-conditional oracle, to fix repeats and skips. (3) Checker-filtered RFT rounds. (4) Step-level GRPO in mlx-vlm with bbox-hit, typed-value and preference rewards. (5) ORPO/DPO on repeat-action negatives. Episode-level online RL is a stretch goal. The total is about 10-14 Mac-hours per user adapter, which fits overnight, plus about 15-25 person-hours of one-time engineering.

### Cited Findings
- Replay amplification precedent: 10 human demos expanded to 200 generated demos reached 79% vs 84% for 200 human demos (MimicGen). — [MimicGen](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.17596)
- Self-filtered fine-tuning gives large relative gains in web environments (Patel +31%; WebRL 4.8% to 42.4%; Agent Q 18.6% to 81.7%). — [Patel](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.20309v2); [WebRL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.02337); [Agent Q](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.07199)
- Tiny-data rule-reward GRPO for GUI actions works: 136 tasks in UI-R1 (+15% action type and +10.3% grounding on AndroidControl, 3B). — [UI-R1 v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.21620v1)
- Expert injection into GRPO when on-policy rollouts fail improved UI-TARS-1.5-7B specifically (22.87% to 32.13% OSWorld-Verified). — [BEPA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.05787v1); replay-buffer variant: [ARPO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.16282)
- LoRA suffices for RL; use a higher LR and an effective batch under 32. — [LoRA Without Regret](https://thinkingmachines.ai/blog/lora/)
- The mlx-vlm GRPO API (`GRPOTrainingArgs`, `group_size`, `beta=0`, custom reward callbacks, sequence limit errors, no multi-turn) is documented. — [mlx-vlm training docs](https://github.com/Blaizzy/mlx-vlm/blob/main/docs/training.md)

### Inferences

**Ranked recipe** (each stage is evaluated on about 50 held-out template instances with both checkers before moving on):

| Rank | Stage | Targets | Data produced | Mac time (est.) | Eng. time (est.) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Baseline eval and prompt/history parity check | all | none | about 0.5 h (50 episodes x 14 steps x 2.5 s, about 30 min) | 1-2 h |
| 1 | **Entity-substituted replay + layout jitter + paraphrase, checker-filtered; SFT** | dates, grounding, preference-step frequency | 5 demos x 40 instances x 2 layouts, about 400 episodes, about 5.6K steps; subsample about 1.5K steps (upweight the 70 originals x3); add about 300 inverse-dynamics aux samples | generation about 0.5-1.5 h (no model, browser only; parallelizable); SFT 1.5K steps x 5 s, about 2.1 h | 6-10 h (selector-based replay, slot extraction, CSS variants, paraphrase templates) |
| 2 | **DAgger with scripted state-conditional oracle** (2 iterations) | repeats ("where am I"), skipped user steps, recovery | per iteration about 60 episodes (cap 25 steps), about 800 visited states labelled free; keep all disagreements plus a sample of agreements, about 400-600 | per iteration rollouts about 60 x 15 x 2.5 s, about 40 min; SFT about 600 steps, about 50 min. Two iterations about 3 h | 4-8 h (progress detection on top of the replay engine) |
| 3 | **Checker-filtered RFT / filtered BC** (1-2 rounds, temperature 0.7, k=4) | robustness, behavioural diversity | 60 instances x 4 = 240 episodes; keep task+preference passes (shortest per instance); hindsight-relabel failures against all checkers | per round about 240 x 35 s, about 2.3 h; SFT about 500 steps, about 45 min | 1-2 h (reuses harness) |
| 4 | **Step-level GRPO in mlx-vlm** (`beta=0`, `group_size` 4, `max_completion_length` 96-128, LoRA r=16, dropout 0) | grounding tolerance, typed-value accuracy, preference steps | about 300 prompt updates on mixed-outcome states from stages 1-3; all-fail states go to SFT with oracle labels (BEPA/ARPO idea) | per update about 4 x 2.5 s generation + about 4 x 5 s backward, about 30 s, so 300 updates is about 2.5 h; plus about 30 min pre-screening | 3-5 h (reward fns, prompt adapter, smoke test on 7B memory and sequence length) |
| 5 | ORPO/DPO on repeat-action negatives (optional, reference-free ORPO) | repeat loops | about 200-400 pairs from DAgger logs | about 0.5-1 h | 1-2 h |
| 6 | Episode-level GRPO with checker reward (stretch; custom loop) | end-to-end success | G=4 episodes per instance | about 2.5 min per group, so 60 groups is about 2.5 h plus updates | 6-10 h |

**Totals per user adapter (estimates):** stages 0-4 take about 10-14 Mac-hours (about 0.5 + 3 + 3 + 3 + 3). That fits one overnight run, or two nights with stage 6. One-time engineering for stages 0-5 is about 15-25 person-hours. Most of it is the selector-based replay and progress-aware oracle, which is reused by every stage.

**Key settings and pitfalls:**
- Upweight preference steps: up to 3x loss weight on the steps the checker marks as preference-relevant. Their frequency is otherwise small relative to generic navigation steps, which is a plausible cause of the "skipped steps".
- Keep a fixed held-out set of instances never used for replay, RFT or GRPO, to measure real generalization rather than template memorization.
- With LoRA at about 2K samples, LoRA capacity is not the bottleneck (LoRA Without Regret). Use rank 16-32 for SFT, rank 8-16 for GRPO, and an effective batch under 32.
- Monitor `grouped_reward_std` in GRPO. If it collapses to 0, the prompts are too easy, so refresh with harder on-policy states.
- Raise `max_seq_length` or drop history screenshots: about 1.3K tokens per 1280x800 image, by my estimate.

**Where each failure mode is attacked:**
- Dates: stage 1 (slot diversity and relative-date paraphrases), stage 4 (typed-value reward), and stage 2 (date-picker states).
- Repeat previous action: stage 0 (history parity), stage 2 (DAgger on loop states), stage 5 (ORPO negatives), and aux inverse-dynamics samples.
- Skipped user steps: stage 1 (preference steps in every replay), loss upweighting, the preference checker in the stage 3 filter, and the preference bonus in stage 4.

### Gaps
- All time estimates are extrapolated from the stated 5 s per train step and 2-3 s per agent step. They are not measured. mlx-vlm GRPO speed and memory on a 7B Qwen2.5-VL with about 1.3-4K-token prompts are unknown, and generation batching inside `group_size` is unconfirmed.
- The Mac's unified memory size is not given. A 7B bf16 policy with G=4-8 activations and grad checkpointing likely needs at least 32 GB; 4-bit QLoRA is the fallback. This is an unverified estimate.
- No published ablation tells us the relative value of stages 1-4 at about 70 seed steps. The ranking is a judgement from the evidence above, not a measured result. A per-stage held-out evaluation is needed to confirm it.
- "Right Screen, Wrong Transition: World Models as Verifiers for GUI Agents" (arXiv 2610.11942, Oct 2026) appeared in search results but was not read. It may offer a learned step verifier as an alternative to element-match rewards.
