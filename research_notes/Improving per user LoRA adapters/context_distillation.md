# Context distillation: moving in-context demonstrations into a per-user LoRA for a GUI agent

Scope: methods that train a model WITHOUT some context to behave like the same model WITH that context (demos, instructions, documents, experience), with evidence and a recipe for our setup (UI-TARS-1.5-7B + per-(user, skill) LoRA in mlx-vlm, 5 demos ≈ 70 step samples, resettable mock apps with checkers).

Legend: "fetched" = I read the paper/page this session. "snippet" = seen only in a search-result snippet or abstract; details not checked. "UNVERIFIED" = from prior knowledge, not re-checked this session; treat IDs/numbers as needing confirmation.

Project numbers used below come from the repo's `STATUS.md` (Nov 21 2026 dry run, oracle demos, Alice × calendar): offline pref-critical steps C1 demos-in-prompt 9/9, C3 LoRA 6/9, C5 other-users' LoRA 0/9; online task success C1 0/3 (wrong relative dates), C3 2/3 (skips 30-min / video / reminder); prompt tokens per step C1 6,814 vs C3 4,114; s/step C1 3.3 vs C3 2.4; training ≈ 25 min per adapter for 70 samples × 4 epochs (≈ 5.4 s/step, batch 1) via the `mlx_vlm.train` CLI (cross-entropy only) in `train/train_adapters.py`.

## Q1. Snell, Klein & Zhong 2022, "Learning by Distilling Context": method, results, and how many examples it needed

### Takeaway
Context distillation trains the student (same model, no context) to match the frozen teacher (same model + instructions/examples/scratchpad) on NEW, model-generated inputs, using a token-level KL loss. With 8 in-context SQL examples it recovered almost all of the ICL teacher's accuracy (27.9 vs 28.2) and beat plain fine-tuning on the same 8 examples by 9 points. It used 4,096 distillation inputs for 1 epoch. That setting is the closest published analogue to our C1 (9/9) vs C3 (6/9) gap.

### Cited Findings
- Paper: Charlie Snell, Dan Klein, Ruiqi Zhong, "Learning by Distilling Context", arXiv:2209.15189 (Sept 2022). Venue: UNVERIFIED (I found no conference version) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.15189), fetched via [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2209.15189)
- **Method (fetched).** Four parts: a raw task-input distribution D, a teacher template (context = instructions, examples or scratchpad), a student template (minimal prompt), and an answer extractor f that strips the scratchpad. The teacher is frozen. The student starts as the same model and is fine-tuned to produce the teacher's final answer from the student prompt — [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2209.15189)
- **Where inputs come from (fetched).** D can be rule-based, unlabeled data, or LM-sampled. In the experiments, raw task inputs were generated by few-shot prompting the same teacher model. So the student is trained on new inputs, not just the original demonstrations — [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2209.15189)
- **Loss (fetched).** The objective is written as log-likelihood of sampled teacher outputs. The authors say hard-label fine-tuning on samples is noisy, so they minimize token-level KL between student and teacher. They approximate the teacher's soft labels with an empirical distribution over 100 sampled tokens per position, to save memory. They report no ablation of KL vs hard labels — [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2209.15189)
- **Hyperparameters (fetched).** 4,096 distillation examples, 1 epoch, batch 16, AdamW, no weight decay. Learning rate 1e-5 (TK-Instruct 11B) or 1e-4 (InCoder-6.7B) — [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2209.15189)
- **Concrete examples (SPIDER text-to-SQL, InCoder-6.7B, exact set match; fetched).**

  | Setting | Teacher (examples in prompt) | Student before distillation | Student after distillation | Fine-tuning on the same examples |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | 4 examples | 27.7 | 0.3 | 22.1 | 13.4 |
  | 8 examples | 28.2 | 0.3 | 27.9 | 18.9 |

  Context distillation beat gradient descent by 8.6 points (4 examples) and 9.0 points (8 examples). The abstract's "9%" is the 8-example number — [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2209.15189)
- **More examples than fit in context (fetched).** With 4 examples fitting in the window, they summed the losses over random 4-of-8 teacher prompts ("simultaneous distillation"). This gave 16.2 ± 0.6 vs 14.22 ± 0.8 before distillation. That is a modest gain and well below the 8-in-context teacher — [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2209.15189)
- **Abstract instructions (Natural-Instructions-V2, 10 tasks, ROUGE-L; fetched).** Teacher 43.4, student before 9.0, student after 34.7, with 11.1× fewer inference tokens. How much explanations helped the teacher correlated with how much the student gained (r = 0.75) — [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2209.15189)
- **Scratchpad (8-digit addition, T5-small; fetched).** Teacher with scratchpad 93%. Distilled student answering directly 95%, so the student beat the teacher. Baselines: transfer 72%, multi-task 61%. Inference used 8.0× fewer tokens. The distillation set was 10k addition examples mixed with 65,536 Natural-Instructions examples, to limit forgetting — [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2209.15189)
- **Overwriting risk (fetched).** In the task-ID experiment, "mixed" distillation overwrote old associations: wrong-association accuracy fell to 16 vs 61 for "naïve". In the fact-editing appendix, the distilled student kept neighbourhood facts worse than the teacher (48 vs 58). So distillation can damage nearby behaviour — [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2209.15189)

### Inferences
- Snell's SPIDER rows have the same shape as our dry run. Teacher with demos ≫ fine-tuning on the demos, and distilling on generated inputs closes most of the gap. The gain over C3 should come from new inputs labelled by the teacher, not from relabelling the same 70 states.
- The data budget was 4,096 inputs for a whole task family. Ours is narrower (one user × one skill, about 9 preference-critical decisions per 3 episodes), so a few hundred step samples is a plausible start (see Q6). That figure is an inference, not a published number.
- Distilling more demos than fit in context (simultaneous distillation over subsets) gave only a small gain. Don't count on it to add demos beyond the 5 text logs that already fit.

### Gaps
- No ablation in Snell of KL vs hard labels, or of the number of distillation inputs. There is no learning curve over the 4,096.
- No multimodal or agentic experiments. Models are ≤ 11B (T5 / TK-Instruct / InCoder).

## Q2. Lampinen et al. 2025, ICL vs fine-tuning generalization, and ICL-augmented fine-tuning

### Takeaway
On matched data, ICL generalizes more flexibly than fine-tuning (reversals, syllogisms, held-out categories). A cheap fix works well: prompt the model with the training data in context, have it write out the inferences (rephrasings, reversals, cross-document links), and add those to the fine-tuning set. "Global" augmentation (whole training set in context) works best. Augmented fine-tuning often matched or beat ICL.

### Cited Findings
- Paper: Andrew K. Lampinen, Arslan Chaudhry, Stephanie C.Y. Chan, Cody Wild, Diane Wan, Alex Ku, Jörg Bornschein, Razvan Pascanu, Murray Shanahan, James L. McClelland, "On the generalization of language models from in-context learning and finetuning: a controlled study", arXiv:2505.00661 (v1 1 May 2025, v3 10 Nov 2025). NeurIPS 2025 FoRLM workshop — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.00661)
- **Main finding (fetched).** In data-matched settings, ICL generalizes several kinds of inference more flexibly than fine-tuning. Fine-tuning can generalize to reversals when they are embedded in a larger structure of knowledge — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.00661)
- **How the augmentation works (fetched).**
  - An LM is prompted with training documents in context and asked to generate inferences, rephrasings or reversals, which are added to the fine-tuning data.
  - **Local:** each sentence is augmented on its own, from a prompt with example rephrasings and reversals.
  - **Global:** the whole training set is in context, and the model links a target document to the rest of the corpus, producing longer reasoning traces. Global generally performed best (App. B.5).

  — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.00661)
- **Sentence splitting (fetched).** Splitting multi-sentence documents into separate fine-tuning examples improved fine-tuning, even controlling for dataset size and steps. It helps with augmentation but can hurt the no-augmentation baseline — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.00661)
- **Results (fetched).** Mostly t-statistics, few raw accuracies.
  - Reversal curse (3,600 training examples): ICL and augmented fine-tuning both reached near-ceiling reversal accuracy, while base fine-tuning was near zero.
  - Semantic-structure benchmark (2,200 documents): augmented fine-tuning "often matched or outperformed" ICL.
  - Category holdouts stayed "very challenging".
  - On the smaller Flash-8B, ICL was worse than vanilla fine-tuning on some splits, but augmentation still helped.

  — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.00661)
- **Setup and cost (fetched).** Gemini 1.5 Flash fine-tuning: batch 8–16, LR 3e-4, 200–1,000 steps. Augmentation took about 3 h on 256 TPU devices for the largest dataset, and the whole study about 2,000 TPU-hours. Only Gemini models were tested — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.00661)

### Inferences
- **Our "global augmentation" analogue.** Put all 5 demos in context with the base model (C1 teacher). For each of the 70 training steps, have it rewrite the `Thought:` to state the cross-demo regularity explicitly. Example: "Alice always sets duration to 30 min and adds video; next I click '30 min'". Then SFT the LoRA on (state, no demos → rewritten Thought + original Action). This targets C3's failure, skipping the 30-min / video / reminder steps.
  - Cost: about 70 teacher calls ≈ 4 min.
  - No new environment states are needed.
  - Ground-truth actions stay unchanged, so grounding can't be damaged.
- Per-step samples (we already split demos into about 70 step samples) are the counterpart of Lampinen's "sentence splitting".
- This is close to our C2 skill file, with one difference: the inferences go into training targets, not the prompt. Lampinen's result suggests that can beat both plain fine-tuning and, sometimes, ICL.

### Gaps
- No exact accuracy tables in what I read (mostly t-statistics). No agents or VLMs.
- No guidance on how many augmentations per example are needed.

## Q3. Other context, prompt and knowledge distillation work (incl. Anthropic-style, system-prompt and generated-knowledge distillation)

### Takeaway
Context distillation is mature, and the evidence has moved in three steps:
1. Off-policy soft-label distillation on generated inputs: Askell 2021, Snell 2022, prompt distillation 2024.
2. On-policy distillation, where the student samples and the teacher-with-context scores: OPCD, SDFT, OPSD, all 2026.
3. Context-shaping fixes for side effects and rare behaviours: EDGE-OPD, PINT, drift analyses, 2026.

Across papers, soft labels and self-distillation (same model as teacher) beat hard-label SFT, and on-policy beats off-policy in most comparisons.

### Cited Findings
**Origins (Anthropic-style context distillation)**
- Askell et al. 2021, "A General Language Assistant as a Laboratory for Alignment", arXiv:2112.00861. This paper introduced "context distillation": fine-tune the model to match its own predictions when prompted with a long HHH (helpful, honest, harmless) prompt, so the prompt can be dropped. It reportedly performed about as well as prompting. UNVERIFIED (not fetched) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.00861)
- Bai et al. 2022, "Training a Helpful and Harmless Assistant with RLHF", arXiv:2204.05862. Reportedly started RLHF from context-distilled models. UNVERIFIED — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.05862)
- Choi et al. 2022, "Prompt Injection: Parameterization of Fixed Inputs", arXiv:2206.11349. Injects a fixed long prompt (persona or task description) into the weights via pseudo-input generation. UNVERIFIED (not fetched; unrelated to "prompt injection" attacks) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.11349)
- Huang et al. 2022, "In-context Learning Distillation", arXiv:2212.10670. Different goal: it transfers few-shot ICL ability from a large teacher to a small student, and does not remove the context. UNVERIFIED — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.10670)
- Padmanabhan et al. 2023, "Propagating Knowledge Updates to LMs Through Distillation", arXiv:2306.09306. The teacher sees an entity definition in context and generates continuations, and the student is trained by KL without the definition. UNVERIFIED — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.09306)
- Guan et al. 2024 (OpenAI), "Deliberative Alignment", arXiv:2412.16339. Completions are generated with the safety spec in the prompt, filtered by a judge, then used for SFT with the spec removed, then RL. This is context distillation with a filter. UNVERIFIED — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.16339)

**Generated-knowledge / prompt distillation into LoRA (2024–2026)**
- **Kujanpää, Marttinen, Valpola, Ilin**, "Efficient Knowledge Injection in LLMs via Self-Distillation" (v1 title "Knowledge Injection via Prompt Distillation"), arXiv:2412.14964 (fetched) — [arXiv HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.14964v2)
  - Setup: teacher = base model with the document in its prompt; student = same model + LoRA, without the document.
  - Data: questions and answers generated at temperature 1.5.
  - Loss: KL at distillation temperature T = 2.
  - Llama-3-8B closed-book results, prompt distillation vs SFT: Amazon 86.1 vs 75.9, NYT 93.6 vs 87.5, Reddit 79.5 vs 69.8. Close to RAG (86.3, 96.3, 78.6).
  - At about 20 questions per document, prompt distillation beat SFT trained on about 10× more data.
  - Lowering T from 2 to 1 cut accuracy (NYT 93.6 → 91.8).
  - Self-distillation beat using a 70B/72B teacher, which the authors attribute to style mismatch. The exception: a 3B student gained from a 14B teacher.
  - Mixing Tülu-3 data limited forgetting (MMLU-Pro 37.2 vs 34.0, base 38.3).
  - Compute: one MI250X for about 24 h (8B, about 10 epochs). Teacher logits can be precomputed.
- Shin et al. 2024, "Generative Context Distillation", arXiv:2411.15927. Prompt internalization via joint training, so explicit prompts aren't needed at inference. Snippet only — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.15927v1)
- "Deep Context Distillation" / Knowledge Modules, arXiv:2503.08727. Distils document knowledge into plug-in LoRA-style modules. Snippet only — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.08727v3)
- Cartridges, arXiv:2506.06266. "Self-study": synthetic conversations about a corpus plus a context-distillation objective, trained into a compact KV-cache representation. Compared against prompt distillation. Snippet only — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.06266)
- Doc-to-LoRA, arXiv:2602.15902. A hypernetwork emits a context-specific LoRA from a document in one forward pass. Snippet only — [arXiv](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2602.15902)

**On-policy context distillation (2025–2026)**
- **Thinking Machines, "On-Policy Distillation"** (Kevin Lu et al., 27 Oct 2025; fetched) — [blog](https://thinkingmachines.ai/blog/on-policy-distillation/)
  - Method: the student samples, the teacher scores every token, and the loss is per-token reverse KL (discount 0).
  - Reasoning: 60% → 70% on AIME'24 in about 150 steps. Estimated 9–30× less compute than extrapolated SFT.
  - Personalization: after mid-training on internal documents, IF-eval fell 85% → 79%. On-policy distillation from the pre-mid-training checkpoint recovered it to 83% while keeping knowledge (36% → 41%).
  - LoRA "learned less" and still forgot.
  - SFT on the model's own samples at temperature 1.0 degraded IF-eval at any non-zero learning rate.
  - The teacher here was an earlier checkpoint, not the model with context.
- **OPCD — Ye, Dong, Wu, Huang, Wei (Microsoft Research)**, "On-Policy Context Distillation for Language Models", arXiv:2602.12275 (v2 23 Mar 2026; fetched) — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.12275)
  - Method: the student generates without context; a frozen teacher (or a shared-weight variant) with context scores it by per-token reverse KL over the student's top-256 tokens.
  - Two uses: distilling "experiential knowledge" (insights the model wrote from its own traces) and system prompts.

  | Task (model) | Context in prompt | Off-policy context distillation | OPCD |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Math (Qwen3-8B; base 75.0) | 77.6 | 78.5 | 79.7 |
  | Frozen Lake (Qwen3-1.7B) | 31.4 | 35.2 | 38.3 |
  | Sokoban (Qwen3-4B-Instruct) | 48.4 | 51.6 | 53.9 |
  | Medical (Llama-3.1-8B) | 72.2 | 75.2 | 76.7 |
  | Safety (Llama-3.2-3B) | – | 83.3 | 83.1 |

  - Safety is the one exception, where off-policy context distillation slightly beat OPCD.
  - Both distilled students beat the teacher with the context in its prompt.
  - Data: 300 experience contexts; 50 training steps × batch 128; LR 1e-6 to 5e-6.
  - Forgetting: OPCD held IF-eval better (Frozen Lake 67.1 vs 65.1 for off-policy distillation).
  - The paper doesn't mention LoRA.
- **SDFT — Shenfeld, Damani, Hübotter, Agrawal (MIT / ETH)**, "Self-Distillation Enables Continual Learning", arXiv:2601.19897 (Jan 2026; ICML 2026 spotlight per listing; fetched). Detailed under Q5 — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.19897), [ICML listing](https://icml.cc/virtual/2026/spotlight/84630)
- **Privileged-information variants (2026).**
  - π-Distill, arXiv:2602.04942: one shared-parameter model whose teacher role sees privileged information. Snippet only — [arXiv](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2602.04942)
  - PINT (NeurIPS 2026): internalizes privileged context into the parameters, reducing OPSD to a context-matched OPD problem. Snippet only — [NeurIPS](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2026/172309)
  - "Privileged Context as Drift in On-Policy Self-Distillation" (NeurIPS 2026): the choice of context sets how far and in which direction the policy moves. Snippet only — [NeurIPS](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2026/165733)
  - "When Context Returns", arXiv:2606.11627: robustness when the context reappears at inference. Snippet only — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.11627)
- **EDGE-OPD** — Lazaridis, Bates, Sharma, King, Lu, FitzGerald, arXiv:2605.23493 (22 May 2026; abstract fetched) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23493)
  - Privileged context (persona, private fact, worked solution) changes the teacher in unintended ways (reasoning, length, style, token preferences), and the student can learn those side effects instead of the target behaviour.
  - Plain OPSD and RLSD failed completely to learn a rare target identity.
  - Two fixes: (a) guided rollouts that inject privileged-context behaviour into the student's sampling, and (b) an evidence mask that updates only tokens where the privileged context supports the sampled token. With guided rollouts, learning succeeded.
  - No numbers in the abstract.
- **"A Predictive Law for On-Policy Self-Distillation From World Feedback"** — He, Sieber, Saponati, arXiv:2605.30070 (28 May 2026; abstract fetched). The student vs self-teacher performance gap at the start of training predicts the final gain linearly, across context types and model families — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30070)

### Inferences
- Results agree on four points (Snell, Kujanpää, SDFT, OPCD):
  - (i) The same model as teacher is fine, and often better than a bigger teacher.
  - (ii) Soft labels beat one-hot labels.
  - (iii) Generating new inputs matters more than reusing the originals.
  - (iv) On-policy usually adds a few points over off-policy and forgets less.
- The predictive law offers a cheap go/no-go test. Measure C1 vs C3 on the new distillation states before training. A small gap there means little to gain.

### Gaps
- None of the Q3 papers is multimodal or GUI. All on-policy context distillation results use full fine-tuning (no LoRA reported), except Thinking Machines, which found LoRA "learned less".
- I didn't verify the numbers of the older (2021–2023) works this session.

## Q4. GUI/web-agent and agent work (2024–2026) that distils demonstrations, memories or workflows into weights

### Takeaway
I found no published GUI-agent paper that runs Snell/SDFT-style context distillation of user demonstrations into weights. Our setup would be new in that respect (searched November 2026, not exhaustive). The closest work:
- GUI papers that use demonstrations both in context and as SFT data (Watch & Learn, AdaptAgent, UI-Mate, LearnAct).
- Text-agent papers that distil accumulated experience into weights by on-policy self-distillation (OPCD's experiential knowledge on games, RSD on ALFWorld/ScienceWorld).

### Cited Findings
- **UI-Mate** (Ding, Dou, Gao et al., arXiv:2608.15930, Aug 2026; abstract fetched) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.15930)
  - Foundation GUI agent (Qwen3.6-27B → UI-MATE-27B) with "in-context demonstration learning": multimodal demos are turned into subtask-level workflows, and the agent re-plans from the live interface.
  - On OSWorkerBench's 33-task self-demo subset, one demonstration raised strict success 17.2% → 35.4% and progress 67.9% → 81.1%. OSWorld-Verified 77.0%.
  - The abstract doesn't say whether the model is trained to use demos or whether demos are distilled into weights. My PDF extraction failed.
- **Watch & Learn**, arXiv:2510.04673. Video-derived trajectories serve both as in-context demos (up to +3 pp on OSWorld) and as SFT data (up to +11 pp for open-weight models). Snippet only — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.04673v3)
- **AdaptAgent**, arXiv:2411.13451. Multimodal human demos are used in context for proprietary models. For open-weight models, meta-learning learns a prior, then a few demos are used for fast adaptation. Snippet only — [arXiv](https://pdf.arxiv.org/pdf/2411.13451)
- **ShowUI-Aloha**, arXiv:2601.07181. One human demonstration is distilled into reusable task guidance, not weights. Snippet only — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.07181)
- **LearnAct** (ACL Findings 2026). Few-shot demonstration learning benchmark and framework for mobile GUI agents. Whether it trains weights from demos: not confirmed (snippet) — [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-acl.1491.pdf)
- **RSD — Zhou, Wu, Peng, Wang, Liu, Fan, Zhang, Zhao**, "Self-Evolving Agents through Privileged Retrospective Distillation", arXiv:2610.27495 (20 Oct 2026; abstract fetched) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27495)
  - The same model is teacher and student. The teacher re-scores the student's actions using privileged retrospective context: precedents and lessons from other tasks, turn-level reflections, and hindsight from the finished trajectory. This is distilled by on-policy self-distillation over several rounds.
  - Results: up to +59.7% relative over base on ALFWorld / ScienceWorld / SearchQA (Qwen3-8B/14B), with no ground-truth trajectories or verifier rewards for the core update. Per-benchmark raw scores weren't in the abstract.
- OPCD's experiential-knowledge results on multi-turn text games (Frozen Lake 31.4 → 38.3; Sokoban 48.4 → 53.9, vs in-context) are the strongest evidence that distilling "experience in context" into weights works for multi-step agents — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.12275)
- RAISED, arXiv:2610.06401. Self-distillation for robustness to prompt injection in LLM agents. Title only, not read — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.06401)
- Background, UNVERIFIED (not fetched):
  - Learn-by-Interact (Su et al., arXiv:2501.10893) uses synthesized agent trajectories both for retrieval-ICL and for training — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.10893)
  - ICAL (Sarch et al., NeurIPS 2024, arXiv:2406.14596) learns abstractions from VLM-agent demos into memory, and reportedly also fine-tunes on them — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.14596)
  - Agent Workflow Memory (Wang et al., arXiv:2409.07429) induces workflows into the prompt, not weights — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.07429)
  - DAgger (Ross, Gordon, Bagnell, AISTATS 2011, arXiv:1011.0686): imitation learning on learner-visited states labelled by an expert — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1011.0686)

### Inferences
- The GUI literature treats "demos in context" and "demos as SFT data" as two separate arms. Training on teacher-with-demos labels at new or student-visited states is a gap we can fill. We can cite OPCD/RSD (text agents) and SDFT/Snell (non-agent) as the closest evidence.
- In agent settings, DAgger is the classic name for "student visits states, teacher labels them". Use that term next to "on-policy distillation" in the write-up, because the two differ (see Q5).

### Gaps
- Couldn't read UI-Mate's full text (PDF extraction failed) to see whether its demo-following is trained in.
- No paper found that does per-USER (personalization) context distillation for GUI agents.

## Q5. Teacher–student variants: new states (on-policy) vs original demo states; KL/logit distillation vs hard-label SFT

### Takeaway
Train on new states, not only the 70 demo states, and prefer states the student itself visits.

| Method | Score (SDFT tool use) |
|---|---|
| On-policy SDFT | 70.6 |
| Offline distillation on teacher samples | > SFT, but < SDFT (exact figure not extracted) |
| SFT on demos | 63.2 |

OPCD beats off-policy context distillation in 4 of 5 tasks.

Soft labels help (Snell; Kujanpää T = 2 > T = 1), but our training path (`mlx_vlm.train`) is cross-entropy only. So start with hard-label DAgger, where the student visits states and the frozen teacher writes the label, and add top-k forward-KL later. Pure reverse-KL OPD on student samples is risky for us. Our key misses are rare actions the student never samples (EDGE-OPD's failure mode).

### Cited Findings
- **SDFT method (fetched).**
  - The same model is student π(·|x) and teacher π(·|x, c), where c is one expert demonstration in the prompt. The template starts "This is an example for a response to the question:" and ends "Now answer with a response of your own, including the thinking process:".
  - The student samples one rollout per prompt; more rollouts gave negligible gains.
  - The teacher is an EMA of the student (α ∈ {0.01, 0.02, 0.05}). A frozen base teacher was stable but underperformed; using the live student as teacher diverged.
  - Loss: derived as reverse KL with an analytic per-token (full-vocabulary) estimator. But the text also says "Forward KL yields the best performance" in practice, an internal inconsistency to note.

  — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.19897)
- **SDFT results, Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, new task / prior-capability average (fetched).**

  | Task | Base | SFT | SDFT |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Science Q&A | 32.1 / 65.5 | 66.2 / 53.4 | 70.2 / 64.5 |
  | Tool use | 42.9 / 65.5 | 63.2 / 56.0 | 70.6 / 65.4 |
  | Medical | 30.1 / 65.5 | 35.5 / 60.2 | 40.2 / 65.4 |

  — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.19897)
- **SDFT knowledge acquisition (fetched).** 2025 events, strict / lenient / OOD: SFT 80 / 95 / 80, SDFT 89 / 100 / 98, oracle RAG 91 / 100 / 100 — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.19897)
- **SDFT ablation, off-policy vs on-policy (fetched, Sec. 4.6).** On tool use, both "SFT on teacher samples" and "offline KL distillation on teacher outputs" beat SFT on demos, but both are below on-policy SDFT. Ranking: on-policy > offline teacher-sample distillation > SFT on demos — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.19897)
- **SDFT scale and cost (fetched).**
  - The advantage grows with scale: about +4 points at 7B, +7 at 14B. At 3B, SDFT lags SFT, because weak ICL gives a poor teacher.
  - Cost: about 2.5× the FLOPs and 4× the wall-clock of SFT.
  - Full fine-tuning (no LoRA), 2 epochs for skills, LR swept 5e-6 to 5e-5.

  — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.19897)
- **SDFT, richer teacher context helps (fetched).** Teacher context of text + answer gave 89% strict vs 75% with text only (App. A.2) — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.19897)
- **OPCD (fetched).** Reverse KL on student samples over the top-256 tokens, with a frozen teacher by default. It beat off-policy context distillation (teacher-generated data, forward KL) in 4 of 5 settings, and forgot less — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.12275)
- **Thinking Machines (fetched).** On-policy distillation gives dense per-token supervision far more cheaply than RL. SFT on the model's own T = 1 samples degraded instruction following. LoRA learned less — [blog](https://thinkingmachines.ai/blog/on-policy-distillation/)
- **Snell (fetched).** KL, not hard labels, because hard labels on samples are "noisy" — [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2209.15189)
- **Kujanpää (fetched).** Self-distillation KL at T = 2 beats SFT at every LoRA rank tested and is about 10× more data-efficient. Lowering T to 1 hurts — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.14964v2)
- **EDGE-OPD (abstract fetched).** Plain on-policy self-distillation "fail[s] completely" to learn a rare target behaviour, because the student never samples it. Guided rollouts that inject the privileged behaviour into sampling fix this — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23493)
- **Other on-policy distillation background, UNVERIFIED (not fetched):**
  - GKD (Agarwal et al., ICLR 2024, arXiv:2306.13649): trains on student-generated sequences with teacher token-level divergences, and generalizes KL choices — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.13649)
  - MiniLLM (Gu et al., arXiv:2306.08543): reverse KL for distillation — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.08543)

### Inferences
- **"On-policy" means two different things for agents. Choose deliberately.**
  - (a) **OPD / OPCD / SDFT style.** The student samples the action tokens, and the teacher scores those tokens (reverse KL). It needs a custom training loop, and the student is pushed only on tokens it already samples (top-k).
  - (b) **DAgger style.** The student's policy chooses which states are visited, and the teacher writes the action at each state. Train on (student prompt, no demos → teacher action), with hard labels or forward-KL soft labels.
  - Our C3 failure is that it never clicks "30 min". That's exactly the rare-behaviour case where (a) gives weak signal (EDGE-OPD) and (b) gives a direct target. So use (b), and optionally mix in some teacher-driven rollouts (DAgger's β-mixture, similar to EDGE-OPD's guided rollouts).
- **Original demo states alone add little.** We already have their ground-truth labels, and C3 trains on them. Teacher relabelling there adds only soft-label information. Keep the 70 ground-truth samples as anchors (upweighted ×2) and add teacher-labelled new states.
- **Which teacher.**

  | Candidate | Strength | Weakness |
  |---|---|---|
  | T1 = base + 5 demos (C1) | Best preference adherence (9/9 offline, 87% online) | Never completed a task online (0/3); grounding worse than C3 |
  | T2 = C3 LoRA + 5 demos | Could combine C3's grounding with demo-following | Untested |
  | EMA-of-student + demos (SDFT style) | Stable in SDFT's experiments | Only stable if T2 behaves well |

  - Measure T2 offline first (42 steps, minutes).
  - SDFT found a frozen teacher stable but weaker, the EMA teacher best, and the live student as teacher unstable. EMA-of-student + demos is a later step, only if T2 behaves well.
- **Hard vs soft labels.**
  - The preference-critical content of a GUI step is a handful of tokens: which element, which typed value.
  - Hard greedy labels capture that directly and work with the existing `mlx_vlm.train` CLI.
  - Soft labels matter most when the teacher is uncertain, which happens at exactly the states where it might be wrong. Store the teacher's top-k (e.g. k = 20) log-probs over the response tokens anyway, so a KL variant can be added later without regenerating data.

### Gaps
- No paper compares DAgger-style hard relabelling with OPD reverse-KL for context distillation on multi-step agents.
- No evidence on context distillation into LoRA specifically for a VLM. Thinking Machines' "LoRA learned less" is the only LoRA data point (and for knowledge, not context).
- How KL over coordinate digits like `(512,284)` behaves for click grounding is unknown. Digit-level distributions may not map to spatial uncertainty.

## Q6. Cost and risk: how many teacher samples, and the teacher copying wrong habits

### Takeaway
Published budgets:

| Paper | Budget |
|---|---|
| Snell | 4,096 inputs × 1 epoch |
| Kujanpää | about 20 generated QAs per document already beat SFT trained on about 10× more |
| OPCD | 50 steps × 128 rollouts ≈ 6,400 |
| SDFT | thousands to about 20K examples, 2–4 epochs |

For one user × one skill, I estimate 300–600 relabelled step-states (about 25–50 new episodes) as a first target, with a learning curve at 100/200/400. That estimate is mine, not published.

The main risks:
- The teacher's systematic errors get baked in: C1's wrong relative dates and weaker clicking.
- Teacher phrasing artifacts ("Following the example…").
- Literal copying of incidental demo details.
- Leaking persona preferences through checker-based filtering, which would invalidate the "learned from 5 demos" claim.

### Cited Findings
- **Sample budgets (fetched).**
  - Snell: 4,096 inputs, 1 epoch — [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2209.15189)
  - Kujanpää: 20 questions per document beat SFT trained on about 10× more — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.14964v2)
  - OPCD: 300 contexts, 50 steps × batch 128 — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.12275)
  - SDFT: about 20K for Medical, 2 (skill) to 4 (knowledge) epochs — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.19897)
  - Thinking Machines: 64 prompts × 4 samples per batch; about 150 steps for the reasoning result — [blog](https://thinkingmachines.ai/blog/on-policy-distillation/)
- **Copying teacher artifacts (fetched).** In SDFT the student copied teacher phrasing like "Based on the text…" / "Following the example…". Masking the loss over the first few response tokens reduced this without hurting accuracy (the authors call it a heuristic) — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.19897)
- **Side effects of privileged context (abstract fetched).** Context changes reasoning, length, style and token preferences, and the student can learn those instead of the target. EDGE-OPD's evidence mask updates only tokens the privileged context actually supports — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23493)
- **Teacher ceiling (fetched).**
  - Distilled students usually reach or slightly exceed the teacher. Examples: OPCD 79.7 vs 77.6 in-context; Snell's addition 95 vs 93.
  - Distillation also inherits the teacher's blind spots. Snell's distilled students kept neighbouring facts worse than the teacher (48 vs 58).
  - SDFT at 3B (weak ICL) did worse than SFT.

  — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.12275), [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2209.15189), [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.19897)
- **Gain predictor (abstract fetched).** The initial student–teacher gap linearly predicts the final gain — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30070)
- **Bigger teacher not better (fetched).** Self-teacher beat a 70B/72B teacher, attributed to style mismatch — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.14964v2)
- **Project evidence (repo `STATUS.md`, local).**
  - C1 does Alice's full procedure but types the wrong date for relative days ("Thursday" → 2026-11-21 instead of 11-19).
  - C0/C2 click about 26 px below Save.
  - C3 learned where Save is and the date pattern.
  - Oracle demos repeat an identical procedure, so they flatter C1. Human demos will vary more.

### Inferences
- **Risk 1: systematic teacher errors (dates, grounding).** Mitigations that leak no preferences:
  - (a) The task generator knows each new instance's target date and title (task facts, not preferences). Drop or correct teacher `type()` labels into date fields that contradict the instance.
  - (b) Use the mock app's DOM/element map to check that the teacher's click lands on a real element. Snap coordinates to the element centre.
  - (c) Agreement rule: if teacher and student target the same element, keep the label (snapped). If they differ, keep the teacher's choice, since that's the preference signal. If the teacher's target isn't a valid element, drop the state.
  - (d) Mix in the 70 ground-truth demo samples.
- **Risk 2: phrasing artifacts.** UI-TARS `Thought:` text from a demo-conditioned teacher will likely say "as in the previous demonstrations…". Filter those phrases with a regex, or mask the first about 10 Thought tokens (the SDFT heuristic). Check rates in the generated data before training.
- **Risk 3: literal over-copying of incidental demo content** (titles, guest names from one demo). Heuristic: flag typed strings that appear in demos but not in the current instruction or screen. Allow them only if they recur in at least 3 of 5 demos, which marks a consistent habit, i.e. a preference. Human demos make this more important.
- **Risk 4: preference leakage via the checker.** `apps/checkers.py` encodes each persona's preferences ("never pass another persona's prefs").
  - If distillation episodes are filtered by the full checker, the adapter gets preference information that isn't in the 5 demos. This is a methodological confound for the C1-vs-C3 claim.
  - Filter only on task-generic parts (saved, correct date/title), or report a checker-filtered variant separately as "with verifier".
  - The same holds for `recorder/oracle.py`: oracle relabelling is a useful upper bound (perfect teacher), not an arm.
  - The new-instance instruction generator must not state preferences either (e.g. no "book a 30-minute meeting").
- **Risk 5: rare behaviours not learned.** Use DAgger relabelling plus some teacher-driven or guided rollouts (Q5).
- **Risk 6: grounding regression of the LoRA.** Measure online task success and click-hit rate on non-preference steps after each round. If they drop, use the context-delta mask:
  - Compute Δ = log p_teacher(token | demos + state) − log p_base(token | state) for each label token.
  - Weight or keep loss only on steps or tokens with large Δ. Those are where the demos changed the decision.
  - Keep original or ground-truth labels elsewhere.
  - This adapts EDGE-OPD's evidence mask; it's my proposal, not tested.

### Gaps
- No published learning curve for "number of teacher-labelled agent states vs gain" in any GUI setting. My 300–600 estimate comes from scaling down Snell/OPCD budgets to a narrow skill.
- Unknown whether the teacher's errors are mostly systematic (date arithmetic) or random. Random errors average out under KL; systematic ones don't.

## Q7. Concrete recipe for our setup, with hours and compute estimate

### Takeaway
Run a staged pilot on Alice × calendar:
- **D0 (cheap).** Lampinen-style Thought augmentation of the existing 70 samples.
- **D1 (main).** One to two rounds of DAgger-style context distillation: student LoRA visits new task instances, a frozen demo-conditioned teacher labels each state, filters, SFT warm-started from C3.
- **D2 (optional).** Top-k forward-KL loss in a custom MLX loop.

Engineering: about 12–16 h for D0 + D1 (plus 6–10 h for D2). Machine time: about 3–4 h per (user, skill) for two D1 rounds, about 10–12 h (one overnight) for 3 personas × calendar, about 30–36 h for all 9 adapters.

### Cited Findings
- New-input distillation > fine-tuning on the same few examples: SPIDER 27.9 vs 18.9 with 8 examples — [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2209.15189)
- On-policy > offline teacher-sample distillation > SFT on demos (SDFT tool use) — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.19897)
- In-context-generated augmentation of fine-tuning data matched or beat ICL in many splits — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.00661)
- Rare behaviours need guided rollouts — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23493)
- Artifact masking heuristic — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.19897)
- KL at T = 2 > T = 1 > hard labels; teacher logits can be precomputed — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.14964v2)
- Initial gap predicts gain — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30070)
- Timing inputs (repo `STATUS.md`): 2.4 s/step (C3), 3.3 s/step (C1 with 6,814 prompt tokens), about 5.4 s/train step (25 min / 280 steps), M5 Ultra 96 GB, UI-TARS-1.5-7B bf16 ≈ 18 GB.

### Inferences
Everything below is my design and estimate, built from the findings above.

#### Step 0: go/no-go measurements (≈ 1 h engineering, ≈ 15 min compute)
1. Add offline arm **T2 = C3 LoRA + demos-in-prompt** (`agent/context.py` + adapter) and score it on the 42-step split next to C1. The teacher is the arm with the best pref-critical score that doesn't regress click accuracy on other steps. If T2 ≥ C1 on pref-critical and ≈ C3 on other steps, use T2. Otherwise use T1 (base + demos) with the grounding filters below.
2. Generate about 10 new task instances, roll out C3, and have the teacher label the visited states. Measure the teacher–student disagreement rate on preference-relevant steps. If it's near zero, distillation has nothing to transfer (predictive law); stop and say so.

#### Step D0: Lampinen-style Thought augmentation (≈ 2 h engineering, ≈ 30 min compute)
- For each of the 70 training samples, prompt the teacher (all 5 demos in context, i.e. "global" augmentation) to rewrite only the `Thought:`. The rewrite states the cross-demo regularity behind the ground-truth action.
- Keep the ground-truth `Action:`.
- Add 1–2 extra paraphrased instructions per demo (instruction rephrasing).
- Train C3-D0 with the existing CLI (same recipe as C3).
- Teacher cost about 70–140 calls × 3.3 s ≈ 5–8 min; training about 25 min.

#### Step D1: DAgger-style context distillation (main arm; ≈ 8–10 h engineering)
1. **New states (2–3 h).** Extend `apps/tasks.py` with a parameterized generator for each skill: new dates and relative-day phrasing, titles, guests, files. Produce about 40 training instances per (user, skill), disjoint from the 3 test tasks and their templates' exact parameters. Instructions must not state any preference.
2. **Rollouts (3–4 h, reusing `agent/run.py`).**
   - Per instance, run the current student (C3, or C3-D0). Use β-mixing: with probability β (about 0.3 in round 1, 0 in round 2), execute the teacher's action instead of the student's. This is the DAgger mixture and plays the role of guided rollouts for rare steps.
   - At every visited state, query the frozen teacher with the student's history plus the 5 demos as text, greedy decoding.
   - Log the label text plus top-20 log-probs per response token (for D2).
   - Stop at the loop detector or the step cap. Drop states after a loop fires.
3. **Filters (2–3 h). No persona-preference information.**
   - Format validity.
   - The click must hit a real element (DOM/element map); snap to the element centre.
   - Typed dates and titles must match the task instance's own facts.
   - Artifact regex on Thought ("demonstration", "example", "previous user…"), or mask the first about 10 Thought tokens.
   - Copied-string rule: incidental strings are allowed only if they recur in ≥3/5 demos.
   - Optional: sample the teacher 2 more times at T = 0.7 and drop states where it disagrees on the target element. This adds about 6.6 s per state.
4. **Training set.**
   - The 70 ground-truth demo samples ×2, plus about 300–500 filtered teacher-labelled states.
   - If there are many more non-preference states than preference-relevant ones, downweight the non-preference ones (or keep them only when teacher and student agree), so grounding is anchored and preferences are taught.
   - Warm-start from the C3 adapter: same rank 16, LR 5e-5, 1–2 epochs, batch 1, existing divergence guard.
5. **Round 2.** Roll out the new student (β = 0) on fresh instances, relabel, add to the pool, retrain or continue.
6. **Evaluate** offline (42 steps, pref-critical split) and online (3 test tasks), against C1, C3 and C3-D0. Also run:
   - An oracle-relabel upper bound: same states, labels from `recorder/oracle.py`. This splits teacher error from distillation loss.
   - A checker-filtered "with verifier" variant, reported separately because it leaks preferences.

#### Step D2 (optional): soft-label distillation (≈ 6–10 h engineering)
- Write a custom MLX training loop, since `mlx_vlm.train` is cross-entropy only. Loss = Σ over response tokens of the top-k teacher distribution (temperature 2 per Kujanpää, renormalized) × −log p_student, plus CE on the 70 ground-truth anchors. The top-k teacher log-probs are precomputed in D1 step 2.
- Optional EDGE-style mask: weight tokens by Δ = log p_teacher(with demos) − log p_base(without).
- Full OPCD/SDFT-style reverse-KL with student sampling inside the training loop costs about 9–10 s per step (student generate ≈ 2.4 s + teacher forward ≈ 1–2 s + forward/backward ≈ 5.4 s). Lower priority, given the rare-behaviour issue.
- Memory: teacher and student as two bf16 copies ≈ 36 GB, which fits in 96 GB. Or toggle the LoRA off for a base-model teacher, if mlx-vlm's LoRA layers allow it (unverified).

#### Compute per (user, skill), from STATUS timings

| Item | Estimate |
|---|---|
| D0 | ≈ 35 min |
| D1 round 1, rollouts + labelling | about 40 instances × 12 steps ≈ 480 states × (2.4 s student + 3.3 s teacher) ≈ 45–50 min (+50 min if 2 extra teacher samples) |
| D1 round 1, training | about 640 rows (70×2 + 500) × 1–2 epochs × 5.4 s ≈ 1–2 h |
| D1 round 2 | about 45 min rollouts + 1 h training |
| Evaluation | offline 42 steps × 5 arms ≈ 15 min; online 3 tasks × 5 arms ≈ 15 min |
| **Total per (user, skill)** | **≈ 3.5–5 h**, two rounds, without extra teacher samples |

At larger scale:
- 3 personas × calendar ≈ 11–15 h, one long overnight via `scripts/overnight.sh`.
- 9 adapters (3 personas × 3 skills) ≈ 30–45 h. Cut this with 1 round, 1 epoch, or about 250 states per adapter (≈ 2 h each, ≈ 18 h total).

#### Engineering total
- D0 + D1 + evaluation wiring ≈ 12–16 h. Plus D2 ≈ 6–10 h.
- First result (Alice × calendar, D0 + D1 round 1) is reachable in one working day plus one overnight run.

#### What to report
- Pref-critical accuracy, online task success, prompt tokens per step, and s/step for C1, C3, C3-D0, C3-D1 (rounds 1 and 2), plus the oracle-relabel upper bound.
- Teacher label accuracy on the distillation states.
- Number of states used, and filter drop rates by reason.
- The core claim to test: whether C3-D1 keeps C3's task success (2/3) while moving pref-critical toward C1 (9/9) at C3's cost (4,114 tokens, 2.4 s per step).

### Gaps
- All hour and compute numbers are extrapolated from one dry run's timings. Image tokens per state and episode length on human demos may differ.
- Whether T2 (LoRA + demos) is a usable teacher is untested. A LoRA trained without context may ignore or misuse prompt demos.
- How well teacher labels hold up on student-visited off-track states is unknown. Measure it via the oracle-relabel comparison.
- With n = 3 test tasks (±30-point CIs on pref-critical), any D1 gain will need more test tasks or personas to be statistically meaningful.
