# Make per-user adapters earn their place

Every component of the planned system already has prior work, so "putting existing parts together end-to-end" will not survive review as a novelty claim. Few-demo LoRA adaptation of a web agent exists (AdaptAgent fine-tunes CogAgent-18B on 2 human demos per new website). LoRA-tuned personalization of a GUI agent exists, but as one model pooled across 95 users (FingerTip 20K). One-LoRA-per-user exists, for text LLMs (OPPU). A per-skill LoRA library with runtime loading exists, for text-only coding agents (Skill-to-LoRA). Turning human GUI recordings into SKILL.md files exists too (Work2Skill). **What no one has published (as of 2026-11-20) is a controlled study, on one GUI-VLM backbone and one user's same 3–5 demos, of whether that user's demonstrated preferences are better carried in adapter weights, in raw in-context demos, or in an induced skill file, under underspecified instructions whose correct answer differs across users.** The recommended framing is therefore an empirical question rather than a system: *where should a user's demonstrated knowledge live?* The (user, skill)-keyed adapter library is the instrument that makes the question answerable. Swapping adapters between users, and training controls at other key granularities, separates personalization from plain skill acquisition, which no prior work does for GUI agents. The literature predicts that a LoRA alone will tie or trail in-context knowledge on accuracy (LatentSkill, Salemi & Zamani, LearnAct), and that weights plus context will win modestly (OPPU, CoAdapt-GUI, ICAL). The design must therefore yield a publishable result either way, and the trust motivation should be restated as an open question, because no CUA study shows that demonstration-based teaching builds trust.

### Trust is a motivation the evidence does not yet support

No CUA study measures whether teaching by demonstration changes trust. The nearest evidence is a 12-person study in which demonstrations captured procedural preferences better than prompts (ALLOY) ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.10049)). Another study found that users want control over subjective preferences, plus approvals only for high-risk actions (Magentic-UI, N=12) ([arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.22358)). A 48-person oversight study found that **users blocked only 21% of problematic actions that reached execution**, and that approving a plan reduced later scrutiny ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.04918v2)). The "teaching increases trust" result is from robotics, not CUAs ([ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/369195572_People_Dynamically_Update_Trust_When_Interactively_Teaching_Robots)). Classic PBD adds a counterweight. CoScripter's deployment showed that reliability failures drove users away ([PDF](https://plunk.org/eben/PublishedPapers/chi-leshed.pdf)), and Lau's retrospective argues that PBD failed on usability, transparency and graceful failure rather than learning power ([AI Magazine](https://ojs.aaai.org//index.php/aimagazine/article/view/2262)). An opaque LoRA is weak on exactly those properties. The defensible move is to motivate the work by *controllability*: a (user, skill) adapter is an explicit, scoped, revocable delegation boundary, which is what the oversight study recommends. Then measure trust-relevant failures programmatically: wrong irreversible actions and over-application of a habit after the user overrides it. Do not claim that trust improves.

## The defensible gap is a matched comparison under conflicting preferences

Stated as narrowly as the evidence allows, the gap is this:

**No published work trains parameter-efficient adapters from a single user's handful (3–5) of recorded GUI demonstrations and compares them, on the same GUI-VLM backbone and with the same demonstrations, against (a) those demonstrations in context and (b) a skill file induced from them, on underspecified tasks whose correct completion differs across users and is checked programmatically.**

Each clause excludes a specific prior paper. "Single user's" excludes FingerTip and PUMA, which pool users. "Recorded GUI demonstrations" excludes OPPU, Skill-to-LoRA and LatentSkill, which are text, as well as SOLO and CoAdapt-GUI, which use self-rollouts. "Same backbone, same demonstrations" excludes AdaptAgent, ICAL and Learn-by-interact, which compare across models ([AdaptAgent](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.13451v1), [ICAL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.14596), [Learn-by-interact](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.10893)). "Skill file" excludes LearnAct, UI-Mate and Work2Skill, which each have only one non-parametric arm and no FT arm ([Work2Skill](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.12984)). "Differs across users" excludes every one-world benchmark (OpenApps, WebArena, REAL), and "checked programmatically" excludes FingerTip's similarity proxy. Two secondary gaps are also real. No benchmark targets desktop-style mail, calendar or drive personalization, since all 2026 personalized benchmarks are mobile or shopping ([iPrefBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.34735v1)). And no paper reports how preference adherence scales with demo count.

Claims to **avoid**: first personalized GUI agent, first personalized GUI benchmark, first LoRA from few demos for GUI agents (AdaptAgent), first skill-LoRA library (Skill-to-LoRA), first per-user LoRA (OPPU). Hedge everything with "to our knowledge," and re-run an arXiv search the night before the poster, since four of the closest threats appeared in the last five months.

## Frame the project as "where should demonstrated preferences live?"

| Framing | Claim | Strength | Weakness |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Controlled study (recommended)** — "Weights, context, or skill file? Where a user's demonstrated preferences should live in a computer-use agent" | On one GUI VLM, matched arms show which carrier of 3–5 demos best resolves underspecified, user-specific decisions. The (user, skill)-keyed library plus swap and granularity controls isolates personalization from skill acquisition. | Fills the exact gap above. Informative whether LoRA wins, ties or loses. Lines up with "Not All Experience Belongs in the Weights" and with ExpActivator's step-position finding. | Needs the non-parametric arms built well, or reviewers will call them strawmen. |
| **B. System** — "PerSkill: a (user, skill)-keyed LoRA library for personalized CUAs" | Record → train in minutes → hot-swap via vLLM multi-LoRA. | Concrete demo for the poster. Cost and latency numbers are easy to measure. | Every part has precedent (Skill-to-LoRA, OPPU, S-LoRA). It reads as the proposal's "integration" claim. |
| **C. Benchmark** — "A few-demo personalization testbed for productivity web apps with conflicting per-user preferences" | 3 personas × 4 skills, seeded personal environments, preference checkers tagged by type. | No desktop or productivity personalized benchmark exists, and the few-demo protocol is new. | Benchmarks are saturated in 2026. Three synthetic personas is small for a benchmark claim. |

**Recommendation: lead with A, present C as the enabling testbed, and present B as the systems appendix and poster demo.** Framing A is how the integration becomes more than the sum of its parts. The library keyed by (user, skill) enables four experiments that none of the component papers could run. First, an **adapter-swap test**: running user A's adapter in user B's environment should reproduce A's preferences. That causally attributes behavior to the adapter and has no analogue in AdaptAgent, FingerTip or Skill-to-LoRA. Second, a **key-granularity ladder**: per (user, skill) vs per user vs per skill across users vs pooled. This separates "learned the skill" from "learned this user," which OPPU's design implies but never tests in agents. Third, a **step-position analysis** of preference-critical steps, which tests ExpActivator's claim that context mainly helps step 1 ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33304)). Fourth, **cost measured in the same stack**: context tokens per step for ICL and the skill file vs adapter swap latency. A typical demo-in-context prompt carries 1,000+ image tokens per screenshot, which is LoRA's most reliable advantage (LatentSkill halved prefill) ([arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.06087)).

For this to work, persona design must make the comparison bite. Give personas **conflicting preferences on the same skill** (e.g., receipts filed to `/Finance/2026` vs `/Receipts`, CC the manager vs never CC, 30- vs 50-minute meetings). Seed **every** persona's environment with the other personas' preferred targets as distractors, so the environment alone cannot reveal the answer. Tag each preference with its PrefDiag type and with whether it can be inferred from the demos, from the environment only, or not at all (ask-only / explicit override) ([PrefDiag-Bench](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.30573), [iPrefBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.34735v1)). Define a step as **preference-critical** when gold actions differ across personas for the same instruction. That definition is objective and lets offline step accuracy be split cleanly.

## Seven essential conditions fit in two weeks on one backbone

All conditions use UI-TARS-1.5-7B, the same 36 test tasks (12 pairs × 3), and identical seeded states, which gives a fully paired design. Adapter counts assume 3 users × 4 skills.

## Hypotheses the prior literature lets you pre-register

| ID | Hypothesis | Grounding | Test |
|---|---|---|---|
| H1 | C3 alone ≤ C1 or C2 on overall online task SR (a tie or loss). | Per-skill LoRA < in-context skill on ALFWorld ([LatentSkill](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.06087)); RAG ≫ PEFT on LaMP ([Salemi](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.09510v2)); 1-demo ICL +14.7 SR ([LearnAct](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.13805v1)) | Paired C3 vs C1/C2 |
| H2 | C3 > C1 on **preference-critical steps after step 1**, with the gap widening by step index. | Context mostly helps the first step ([ExpActivator](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33304)); locators and lessons belong in weights ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01787)) | Step-index × condition interaction, offline |
| H3 | C4 (LoRA + SKILL.md) ≥ max(C2, C3), by a small margin. | OPPU+RAG +2.48% over RAG; CoAdapt-GUI joint 45.0 vs 37.5; ICAL FT+retrieval best | Paired C4 vs best single arm |
| H4 | C3 ≈ C5 on task SR but C3 ≫ C5 on preference adherence. | Personalization must be separated from domain adaptation ([OPPU](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.04401v3)) | Decomposition: SR vs adherence |
| H5 | C6 (pooled, no key) follows the majority or an arbitrary persona's preference on conflicting items, near chance. | FingerTip needed in-context user history to personalize a shared LoRA ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.21071v2)) | Adherence on conflict-tagged items |
| H6 | Under C7 (swap), the agent follows the **adapter owner's** preference in most preference-critical steps. | Skill-to-LoRA's wrong-adapter penalty ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.16769)) | Preference-attribution rate |
| H7 | LoRA arms over-apply habits under explicit overrides, and on ask-only items, more than C2. | SFT memorizes ([Chu et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.17161)); iPrefBench ask-only items | Over-application rate |
| H8 (nice) | LoRA degrades more than C2 under layout perturbation. | SFT memorizes positions; PBD brittleness | N6 |
| H9 (nice) | The LoRA–ICL gap favors LoRA more on UI-TARS than on Qwen2.5-VL. | UI-TARS ICL saturates at 1 demo; Qwen2-VL keeps improving ([LearnAct](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.13805v1)) | N5 |

H1 is the result most likely to occur, so the paper's value must not depend on refuting it. If H1 holds while H2, H3, H4 and H6 also hold, the story is coherent and novel: weights carry *who you are* at decision points mid-trajectory, context carries *what to do*, and the combination is best.

## Conclusion

The research changes the project's center of gravity. The proposal treated the adapter library as the contribution and the baselines as decoration. The literature says the reverse: the library is commodity infrastructure, and the matched comparison against context and skill files is what no one has run for GUI agents. That comparison only means something when users disagree, so conflicting-preference persona design and the swap and other-user controls are the real deliverables, more than any single accuracy number. The likeliest outcome, that weights alone do not beat context but carry user identity at mid-trajectory decision points and combine well with a skill file, would be a useful workshop result precisely because text-personalization studies have reported it but no GUI-agent study has tested it.

A second implication concerns what to build first. Every risk that could sink the project (the zero-shot floor, coordinate mismatch, checker bugs, test/train leakage) shows up in the first three days, before any LoRA is trained. Order the build so that C0, the gold-replay test and checker validation come first. The adapters are the cheapest part of the system, and they will not be the bottleneck.
